# Urdu glossary

The words the Urdu site uses for its key terms, so every page says the same
thing the same way. Use it when translating new text, and when reviewing
`docs/urdu-review.csv`.

## How the Urdu should sound

- **Simple, warm, everyday Urdu**, the way an older sister or a teacher you
  trust would explain things. Short sentences. No Persianised or formal
  words where a common one exists.
- **Speak to the reader as آپ**, and use **feminine verb forms**
  (آپ کر سکتی ہیں، آپ اچھی ہیں), because the site is written for girls.
- **Keep English where people already say it in English**: university,
  scholarship, fee, hostel, merit, online, link, email. Write it in Urdu
  letters (یونیورسٹی، اسکالرشپ، فیس). Don't invent a pure-Urdu word nobody
  uses.
- **Proper names stay in English**: university, scholarship, programme and
  organisation names (Quaid-i-Azam University, PEEF, BS Computer Science),
  cities in addresses, websites, emails and social handles. On the page
  they are marked as English so they read left to right.
- **Abbreviations stay in Latin letters**: HEC, BS, MBBS, FSc, CNIC,
  B-Form, PDF, NTS, MDCAT.
- **Numbers and dates use Western digits** (0–9), as on official
  Pakistani websites and forms: ۲۰۲۶ is not used; 2026 is.
- **Punctuation**: the Urdu full stop ۔ ends a sentence, ، separates items
  in a list and ؟ ends a question.
